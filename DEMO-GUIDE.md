# Demo Guide

Every team gets a short demo. The goal is clarity, not polish.

## Demo format

Aim for **three minutes**:

1. **Problem — 30 seconds**
   - Who is this for?
   - What problem did you choose?

2. **Build — 90 seconds**
   - Show the prototype, workflow, mockup, or experiment.
   - Demonstrate the most important path.

3. **Lesson — 30 seconds**
   - What worked?
   - What surprised you?
   - What is still rough?

4. **Next step — 30 seconds**
   - What would you build next with another day or week?

## Before your demo

- Decide who will speak and who will drive.
- Start from the page or command you need.
- Close unrelated tabs and windows.
- Have a backup screenshot or recording if the live demo is fragile.
- Make sure any credentials or private data are hidden.
- Keep the explanation understandable to someone outside your team.

## What we are looking for

There is no single winning formula. Strong demos usually have:

- A clear person and problem
- A result people can see or understand
- A thoughtful use of AI or automation
- Evidence that the team tested an assumption
- Honesty about limitations
- A useful or surprising next step

A rough project can still be a great demo. Do not spend the final minutes pretending a broken feature works.

## Recognition

Organizers may recognize projects for categories such as:

- Most useful
- Most surprising
- Best use of AI
- Best demo
- Best documented failure
- Most likely to become a real project

These are informal recognitions, not a ranking of people's worth or technical ability.
